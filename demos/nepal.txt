Nepal is a landlocked country in South Asia, wedged between China to the north and India to the south, east, and west. The country rises from the steamy Terai lowlands, barely sixty meters above sea level, to the summit of Mount Everest at 8,849 meters. Eight of the world's fourteen peaks above eight thousand meters lie wholly or partly in Nepal. Mount Everest is called Sagarmatha in Nepali and Chomolungma in Tibetan. Tenzing Norgay and Edmund Hillary made the first confirmed ascent of Everest on 29 May 1953. Annapurna was the first eight-thousand-meter peak ever climbed, by a French expedition in 1950. The Himalaya are still rising by a few millimeters every year as the Indian plate pushes into Asia. The Kali Gandaki gorge, between Dhaulagiri and Annapurna, is among the deepest river valleys on Earth. Glacial lakes such as Imja Tsho are growing as the Himalayan glaciers melt, raising the risk of outburst floods. Rivers like the Koshi, Gandaki, and Karnali carry Himalayan snowmelt south to the Ganges. The middle hills are terraced for rice, millet, and maize, carved into slopes over centuries.

Kathmandu Valley was ruled for centuries by the Newar kingdoms of Kathmandu, Patan, and Bhaktapur. Prithvi Narayan Shah of Gorkha unified dozens of small kingdoms into modern Nepal in the eighteenth century. The Anglo-Nepalese War of 1814 to 1816 ended with the Sugauli Treaty and the loss of territory to the British East India Company. The Rana prime ministers ruled Nepal as hereditary strongmen for more than a century until 1951. Nepal was never colonized, which is a point of deep national pride. The Maoist insurgency from 1996 to 2006 cost more than seventeen thousand lives. The Comprehensive Peace Agreement of 2006 ended the civil war and brought the Maoists into politics. Nepal abolished its 240-year-old monarchy and became a federal democratic republic in 2008. The constitution promulgated in 2015 divided the country into seven provinces. The royal massacre of 2001 killed King Birendra and much of the royal family in a single night.

Nepal is home to more than one hundred and twenty ethnic groups and speaks over one hundred languages. Nepali, written in the Devanagari script, is the official language and a lingua franca across the country. The Newars of Kathmandu Valley created a rich urban culture of pagoda temples, woodcarving, and festivals. The Sherpa people of the Khumbu region are renowned as high-altitude mountaineers and guides. The Tharu people of the Terai have lived in the malarial lowland forests for generations. Gurkha soldiers from the hills have served in the British and Indian armies for over two hundred years. Dal bhat, lentil soup with rice, is the everyday meal eaten twice a day in most homes. Momos, steamed dumplings brought over the mountains from Tibet, are now a beloved street food. Nepal's flag is the only national flag in the world that is not a rectangle, formed from two stacked pennants.

Lumbini, in southern Nepal, is the birthplace of Siddhartha Gautama, who became the Buddha. Hinduism is practiced by most Nepalis, and Buddhism by about one in ten, with traditions often blended together. Pashupatinath temple on the Bagmati River is one of the holiest shrines of Shiva in the world. Swayambhunath, the monkey temple, has watched over Kathmandu from its hilltop stupa for more than two thousand years. Boudhanath is one of the largest stupas in the world and a center of Tibetan Buddhism. The Kumari is a young girl worshipped as a living goddess in Kathmandu until she reaches puberty. Dashain is the longest and most important festival, celebrating the victory of the goddess Durga over evil. During Tihar, the festival of lights, people honor crows, dogs, cows, and brothers on successive days. Holi splashes the streets with colored powder to welcome spring. Buddhist prayer flags carry mantras on the wind from mountain passes and rooftops.

Chitwan National Park protects one-horned rhinoceroses, Bengal tigers, and gharial crocodiles in the lowland jungle. Nepal nearly tripled its wild tiger population between 2010 and 2022, one of conservation's great success stories. Sagarmatha National Park, around Everest, is home to snow leopards, red pandas, and Himalayan tahr. Community forestry handed forests to local user groups and helped bring back tree cover across the hills. Rhododendron, the national flower, paints the hills red and pink every spring. Nepal's elevation range packs tropical, temperate, and alpine ecosystems into a strip less than two hundred kilometers wide.

Tourism, especially trekking and mountaineering, is a major source of income and jobs. The Annapurna Circuit and the Everest Base Camp trek draw walkers from around the world. Remittances from Nepalis working abroad make up about a quarter of the country's economy. Hydropower from Himalayan rivers is Nepal's greatest untapped resource, with potential to export electricity to India and Bangladesh. The devastating Gorkha earthquake of April 2015 killed nearly nine thousand people and toppled historic temples. Reconstruction after the earthquake rebuilt many heritage sites in Kathmandu, Patan, and Bhaktapur. A young, fast-growing technology sector is building startups, digital payments, and software exports from Kathmandu.
