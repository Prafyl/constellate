The Sun is a middle-aged yellow dwarf star that holds more than 99.8 percent of the mass in the solar system. Nuclear fusion in the Sun's core turns hydrogen into helium and releases the energy that lights every planet. Solar flares and coronal mass ejections can disrupt satellites and power grids on Earth. The solar wind is a stream of charged particles that flows outward and carves out a bubble called the heliosphere.

Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun, with almost no atmosphere to hold heat. Venus is wrapped in thick clouds of sulfuric acid, and its runaway greenhouse effect makes it the hottest planet. Earth is the only world known to host life, with liquid water covering about seventy percent of its surface. Mars is a cold desert planet with the tallest volcano in the solar system, Olympus Mons. Rovers such as Curiosity and Perseverance search Martian rocks for signs of ancient microbial life.

Jupiter is a gas giant so large that more than a thousand Earths could fit inside it. The Great Red Spot on Jupiter is a storm wider than Earth that has raged for centuries. Saturn's rings are made of countless pieces of ice and rock, some as small as dust and some as large as houses. Uranus spins on its side, probably because of a giant collision early in its history. Neptune has the fastest winds in the solar system, reaching more than two thousand kilometers per hour.

Europa, a moon of Jupiter, hides a salty ocean beneath its icy shell that may be able to support life. Titan, Saturn's largest moon, has lakes and rivers of liquid methane under a thick orange haze. Enceladus shoots geysers of water vapor into space from cracks near its south pole. Io is the most volcanically active body in the solar system, heated by Jupiter's powerful tides. Earth's Moon probably formed from debris after a Mars-sized body slammed into the young Earth.

The asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter contains millions of rocky bodies left over from planet formation. Ceres is the largest object in the asteroid belt and is classified as a dwarf planet. Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006 after astronomers found similar icy worlds in the Kuiper Belt. The Kuiper Belt is a ring of frozen bodies beyond Neptune, and it is the source of many short-period comets. Comets grow glowing tails of gas and dust when sunlight heats their ice as they approach the Sun. The Oort Cloud is a distant shell of icy objects thought to surround the solar system far beyond Pluto.

The Voyager 1 probe, launched in 1977, crossed into interstellar space in 2012. The James Webb Space Telescope observes infrared light to study the first galaxies and the atmospheres of distant planets. Astronomers have confirmed more than five thousand exoplanets orbiting other stars. The habitable zone is the range of distances from a star where liquid water could exist on a planet's surface.
